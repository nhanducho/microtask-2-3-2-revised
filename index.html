Where should the customer order enter the supply chain?

The decoupling point separates forecast-driven activity from customer-order-driven activity. Its best position depends on speed, customization, and inventory risk.

Upstream

Before production

Most production begins after the order. Typical model: Make to Order.

Middle

Before final assembly

Standard modules are stocked, but final configuration waits. Typical model: Assemble to Order.

Downstream

At finished-goods stock

Complete products exist before the order. Typical model: Make to Stock.

Three businesses, three appropriate positions

The best position changes with product variety and customer lead-time expectations.

Key takeaway

Moving the point changes speed, variety, and risk.

There is no universally correct location. The point should fit the business model and customer expectations.

  1. 1

    Downstream: faster delivery

    More activities are forecast-driven. This suits standard products but requires more finished-goods inventory.

  2. 2

    Middle: balance speed and variety

    Standard modules are prepared in advance while final configuration waits for the order.

  3. 3

    Upstream: greater customization

    More activities wait for customer specifications, reducing finished-goods risk but increasing lead time.

Decision rule

Move the point downstream for speed. Move it upstream for customization. Choose the middle when modularity allows both.
